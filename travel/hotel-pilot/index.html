01 · What this is

A back-office assistant, not a replacement.

Think of it as a well-briefed junior colleague who prepares things and never presses send. It reads what you give it, drafts what a person would draft, and says so plainly when it doesn't know something. Your team keeps every decision and every guest conversation that matters.

What it helps with

  • A clear start to the day: what needs a person, what can wait
  • Sorting the inbox so the important few stand out
  • First drafts of replies in your hotel's voice
  • Welcome notes before guests arrive
  • A short weekly summary of how marketing is doing

What it does not do

  • Send emails or messages to guests
  • Change rates, availability or bookings
  • Touch payments or card details
  • Make up facts, prices or recommendations
  • Handle complaints, refunds or sensitive situations

02 · How a day runs

A morning that starts organised.

This is how a day could run once the pilot is under way. Times are an illustration. Your team sets the real ones.

  1. 01
    Before the team arrives

    The morning briefing is ready

    One short page: what needs a decision today, what came in overnight, and anything the agents couldn't check.

  2. 02
    Early

    The inbox is sorted

    Booking inquiries, requests for recommendations and supplier notes are grouped. Anything sensitive is flagged for your General Manager, and suspicious messages are marked "do not reply".

  3. 03
    Through the morning

    Drafts are prepared

    Replies and welcome notes appear as drafts. Anything the agent can't confirm is marked [CONFIRM] for a person to fill in, such as rates, availability or policy details.

  4. 04
    Your team

    A person reviews and approves

    Someone edits, approves and sends from your usual tools. Nothing leaves the building without a named approver.

  5. 05
    Always

    Everything is logged

    Each run records what went in, what came out and who approved it, so there's always a trail to look back on.

03 · The agents · Phase 1

Nine helpers to start. Each with limits.

Each agent has one job, a short list of things it must never do, and a clear rule for when to hand over to a person. Agent designs are adapted from the open-source TH1.ai Agent Stack (MIT licence).

Daily

Morning briefing

Pulls the day together on one page: what needs you, what was sorted, what couldn't be checked.

Never: claims it checked something it didn't.

Email

Inbox sorter

Groups messages and ranks them so you see the few that matter, and flags suspicious ones.

Never: replies, deletes or moves email.

Email

Email drafter

Writes one-off emails in your hotel's voice for you to edit and send.

Never: drafts replies to complaints, refunds or sensitive messages.

Email

Booking inquiries

Drafts a warm, personal reply to a new inquiry, using only facts you've approved.

Never: quotes a rate or promises a room.

Guest experience

Concierge

Suggests restaurants, tours and transfers from your list of vetted local contacts.

Never: recommends anyone who isn't on your list, or books for a guest.

Guest experience

Welcome notes

Prepares a friendly pre-arrival message for upcoming guests.

Never: sends it, or mentions personal details the guest didn't share.

Reporting

Weekly marketing summary

A two-page plain-language look at traffic, ad spend and occupancy from the numbers you export.

Never: fills in a missing number, or claims credit for results.

Research

Web researcher

Looks things up online and cites where each answer came from.

Never: adds to your approved lists, or follows instructions found on a web page.

Visibility

AI-search visibility

Prepares clear answers and structured data so AI search tools describe the hotel correctly.

Never: publishes anything, or includes reviews, awards or rates.

04 · The whole package

Phase 1 is the start. Here's the full picture.

The nine agents above are the first step. The rest are offered as later stages, each one opened up only when the hotel is ready and has agreed to it. The later stages are a proposal. They are not built or connected to any system yet, and each one would be fitted to the hotel's own booking and property systems.

Stage 2a · Read-only data
They look. They don't change anything.

Needs a written data-handling agreement and read-only access to reservations and email.

Guest experience

Arrival-time check

Spots upcoming arrivals with no arrival time and drafts a polite note to ask.

Needs: read access to reservations.

Never: sends the note, or contacts a guest.

Housekeeping

Daily housekeeping plan

Prepares a draft room plan from the day's arrivals and departures.

Needs: read access to reservations.

Never: changes a room's status or assigns staff.

Reporting

Hotel analytics

A read-only summary of occupancy and revenue so performance is clear at a glance.

Needs: read access to reservation reports.

Never: edits data, or fills in a number it doesn't have.

Email

Continuous email drafting

Keeps drafting replies through the day so nothing waits. A person approves each one.

Needs: read access to the inbox.

Never: sends anything.

Stage 2b · Prepares proposals
They propose. A person decides and applies.

Needs sign-off from whoever owns revenue and sales, and access to the relevant systems.

Revenue

Rate proposals

Proposes rate and minimum-stay changes for the next 30 days, with the reasoning shown.

Needs: revenue sign-off and channel or booking system access.

Never: applies a rate or changes availability.

Revenue

Upgrade offers

Finds upgrade opportunities and prepares the offer and the room move for a person to approve.

Needs: reservation access and your upgrade rules.

Never: moves a guest or sends an offer.

Sales

Sales pipeline

Tracks corporate and group inquiries from first contact on, and drafts follow-ups for ones going quiet.

Needs: a CRM or shared sheet, and a data agreement.

Never: contacts anyone on its own.

Partners

Partner alerts

Where the hotel works with travel partners, drafts a note when rates or availability move.

Needs: your partner list. Only useful if you have partners to notify.

Never: sends the note.

Stage 3 · Money records and food
Handled last, with the most care.

Needs finance access and a stricter data agreement. Breakfast planning applies only if the hotel prepares breakfast itself.

Finance

Receipt filing

Reads receipts and invoices and prepares them for filing and month-end.

Needs: finance access and a stricter data agreement.

Never: pays, approves or moves money.

Food and beverage

Breakfast planning

If breakfast is prepared in-house, turns expected guest numbers into a draft shopping list.

Needs: guest counts and your inventory list.

Never: places an order.

Every later agent follows the same guardrails as Phase 1: drafts and proposals only, a named person approves, sensitive topics go to the General Manager, and everything is logged.

05 · The guardrails

Where the agents stop.

  1. 01

    People approve, always

    Agents draft and propose. Every outward action needs a named person's approval, recorded with the time.

  2. 02

    Sensitive topics go to your General Manager

    Complaints, refunds, safety, legal matters, media, accessibility and guests in distress get no AI reply. They are flagged straight away with a short, neutral summary.

  3. 03

    No guessing

    Agents answer only from facts your hotel has confirmed. If they don't know, they say so and ask. No invented amenities, prices, policies or local tips.

  4. 04

    No fake proof

    No made-up reviews, testimonials, guest names or quotes, anywhere, including mock-ups.

  5. 05

    No live system changes

    In this pilot the agents don't connect to your booking or property management system, and can't change a rate or a reservation.

07 · Privacy

Guest information is treated carefully.

How we handle it

  • Canadian privacy law applies (PIPEDA and BC's PIPA).
  • We collect only what a task needs.
  • No real guest data is used until a written data-handling agreement is in place with the hotel. Early work uses made-up examples.

What we never store

  • Payment card details. They're removed from logs.
  • Passwords or access keys in our code.
  • Guest details in outside search queries.

Where data would be stored and who can see it will be agreed in writing before any real guest information is used.
